Judgement · Peer-to-peer. No server, no accounts.

How to play

Judgement — also called Kachuful, or Oh Hell — is a trick-taking game for three to seven players. You are scored on predicting exactly how many tricks you will take, not on taking as many as possible.

A round

Everyone is dealt the same number of cards. A trump suit is set. Each player says how many tricks they think they will win. Then the hand is played out, and you are scored on whether you were right.

The round sizes change as the match goes: down from the biggest hand the deck allows to a single card and back up again, or just the one descent, depending on the shape the host picked.

Bidding

Bids go round in turn and are public the moment they are made — you can see what everyone before you has said, and they can see yours once you submit it.

The dealer bids last. If the host has the hook switched on — it is on by default — the dealer may not make the bids add up to the number of tricks available. Somebody at the table then has to be wrong, which is what stops a round where everybody quietly gets what they asked for. With the hook off, the dealer may bid anything and a whole table can make it.

Playing

Play moves clockwise. The winner of a trick leads the next one.

You must follow the led suit if you can. If you cannot, you may play anything, including a trump. The highest trump wins the trick; if no trump was played, the highest card of the led suit wins.

Trump

  • Rotation — a fixed cycle, so everyone knows the trump before the deal.
  • Turned card — the top card of what is left is flipped after the deal and its suit is trump.
  • Rotation with No Trump — as rotation, but every fifth round has no trump at all and the led suit is the only thing that wins.

Scoring

You score only if you take exactly what you bid. The host picks which way that pays:

  • Kachuful — ten times your bid. A made bid of four scores 40. Missing costs nothing, so bid boldly. A made zero pays five times the round size, which makes ducking a ten-card round worth 50.
  • Standard — ten plus your bid. A made four scores 14, a made zero scores 10. Ambition buys you almost nothing; accuracy buys you everything.
  • Squared — ten plus your bid squared, so a made four scores 26 — and missing takes points away: the square of how far out you were, and nothing else. Out by one costs 1, out by two costs 4, out by three costs 9. Over and under cost the same, so once you have made your bid an extra trick hurts exactly as much as one you failed to take. The only mode where a score can go negative, and the only one where the timid bid is sometimes the right one.

Playing together

One person hosts and shares the four-character room code; everyone else joins with it. The connection is directly between your devices — there is no server holding the game.

If your phone dies or your browser closes, rejoin with the same code on the same device and you get your seat and your full scoreboard back. The host's device is the one that must stay: if it leaves, the match ends.
